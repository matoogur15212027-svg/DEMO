RESEARCHER NOTES

From idea to a testable experience.

A feasibility prototype for discussion with your professor. This is not a validated measurement instrument or a recruitment-ready study.

Research question

How does offering tenants a choice between an AI assistant and a property manager, compared with mandatory AI-first contact, affect their willingness to use AI for maintenance requests?

Two assigned conditions

A: everyone starts with LEA, which collects three facts before a property manager takes over. B: tenants choose the same LEA route or direct human contact. Assignment is random at consent, with an independent 50/50 draw; small samples will not necessarily be balanced.

Primary and secondary outcomes

Proposed primary outcome: stated willingness to use LEA (1–7), measured after the service policy is shown and before route selection or waiting. Perceived control is measured at the same point. These are post-treatment measures, not a pre-treatment baseline. Asking these questions may prime the later choice; discuss this trade-off with your professor.

Secondary outcomes after the interaction: satisfaction, expectations, willingness for a future request, control, perceived progress, effort and perceived waiting time. All items are custom pilot items. They must be reviewed and, where appropriate, replaced with validated scales before confirmatory data collection. Do not combine them into a score without a measurement rationale.

What the experience controls

The same contained, non-emergency leak; the same three qualification questions and supplied answers; the same manager, appointment question and final appointment confirmation. Both characters are scripted; there is no live LLM. The problem is handled by scheduling a repair, not physically repaired during the chat. Neutral fictional branding avoids claiming affiliation with OQORO.

Waiting times

The requested pilot uses 45 seconds after AI qualification versus 90 seconds before direct human contact. Demo uses 3 versus 6 seconds. The original brief mentioned 90 minutes; 90 seconds is a provisional assumption for feasibility, to confirm with the researcher. Settings support 0–600 seconds. The queue clock starts after qualification for the AI route and immediately after selection for the human route. Thus equal queue settings do not guarantee identical total interaction time.

A comparison of satisfaction between assigned conditions estimates the overall effect of offering these service options, including the different journeys participants select. It does not isolate a pure psychological choice effect independent of waiting time or channel. Comparing people who select AI with those who select a human is not a randomised comparison.

Suggested analysis

Compare willingness after policy presentation between all randomly assigned A and B participants. Compare final satisfaction by assigned condition as a secondary outcome. Report route uptake within B descriptively. Keep forced runs and demo runs out of participant analysis. Do not pool different wait configurations. Record exclusions and rules before analysis; the choice-comprehension check should not automatically trigger exclusions after seeing results.

For a cleaner choice-focused follow-up, standardise the information about delays and the time from first contact to manager arrival. Alternatively, use a separate factorial design for choice policy and waiting-time policy. The current equal-wait button only equalises queue durations.

Feasibility checks before recruiting

Try all three paths: mandatory AI, choice → AI, and choice → human. Check comprehension, perceived realism, duration and questionnaire wording with a few pilot participants. A single test demonstrates functionality, not an effect. Determine sample size with the professor using a planned primary comparison and a justified effect-size assumption.

Data and practical limits

This static prototype saves completed responses in local browser storage only; it does not collect centralised data from remote participants. JSON exports contain event timestamps and scripted dialogue; CSV contains one row per completed run. Browser reloads resume an unfinished run when session storage is available. Refresh during a questionnaire can lose unsubmitted answers. Tab visibility events help identify interruptions; timers can overrun if a tab is suspended. Actual waits are recorded.

No names or contact details are required. An optional comment could still contain personal information. Withdrawal before submission removes the unfinished local session. Participants can delete their completed response locally; downloaded copies cannot be remotely removed. Browser storage can be cleared or blocked. Export after each supervised pilot session. The researcher panel is a convenience view, not a secure access-control boundary.

Before a real study: obtain the relevant university approval, finalise consent and retention arrangements, review scales and hypotheses, implement central collection if needed, and restrict participant access to researcher controls. Private hosting initially allows only the site owner; a copied link does not automatically grant access.
